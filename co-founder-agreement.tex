\subsection{The Anti-Loafing Protocol}
\label{sec:antiloafing}

Loafing is defined as sustained failure to meet the minimum contribution standards above, without an agreed leave of absence, for two or more consecutive quarters.

When loafing is identified, the following process is to be followed in order. Neither founder may skip a step.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Private conversation}: the other founder raises the concern directly and privately. A 30-day improvement period is agreed with specific, measurable targets. This conversation is to be documented.
  \item \textbf{Formal written notice}: if improvement targets are not met after 30 days, a written notice is issued citing specific failures and setting a final 60-day improvement period with milestones. Signed by both founders.
  \item \textbf{Equity vesting pause}: if the 60-day period passes without meeting agreed milestones, vesting for the underperforming founder is paused from that date. The pause continues until minimum standards are met consistently for one full quarter.
  \item \textbf{Forced buyout}: if vesting remains paused for more than \blank[2cm] months with no improvement, the performing founder may invoke a forced buyout of the underperforming founder's unvested shares at original issue price. Vested shares are retained per the Good Leaver provisions in the Departure section.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{warnbox}
\textbf{Important:} This process is not punitive --- it is protective. It protects the company, protects the working founder from carrying dead weight, and protects the underperforming founder from being accused without due process. Both founders are to agree to submit to this process if it is ever invoked against either of them.
\end{warnbox}

\subsection{Board of Directors}

\subsubsection{What a Board of Directors Is}

A Board of Directors is a formal governing body with legal authority over the company. Unlike advisors, directors have fiduciary duties --- they are legally accountable for major company decisions. In a private company, the board typically approves major financial decisions, appoints and can remove the CEO, and signs off on equity issuances and fundraising rounds.

At the current stage, EduReach does not need a formal board. The founders are the directors. However, as the company grows and investors come in, a board will be formed --- and if it is not structured carefully, founders can lose control of their own company.

  \item Board observer rights (the right to attend board meetings without voting) may be offered to early-stage investors as a concession in place of a full board seat
  \item Any board resolution that affects founder equity, CEO authority, or company dissolution requires a supermajority of \blank[2cm]\% of the board

\subsubsection{Independent Director}

An independent director is a neutral board member who is not a founder, investor, or employee. They are often an experienced entrepreneur, executive, or lawyer. Their role is to:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Break deadlocks between the two founders
  \item Provide experienced oversight of major decisions
  \item Increase investor confidence and company credibility
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Equity: 0.1--0.25\% (from the ESOP), vesting over 2--3 years
  \item A modest director's fee once the company is funded (to be agreed at appointment)
  \item A signed Directors' Agreement covering duties, liability, and term

  \item Liquidation preferences above 1x non-participating (this means in a sale, the investor gets their money back first before founders receive anything --- the more aggressive versions can leave founders with nothing)
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{How to Handle Multiple Investors}

As the company grows, there will be multiple investors on the cap table. Both founders are to agree:

\begin{itemize}
  \item A lead investor is to be identified for each round --- they set terms, others follow
  \item Side letters (private additional terms given to one investor that others do not know about) are to be avoided entirely
  \item All investors in a given round are to receive the same terms
  \item A cap table management tool is to be used from the first investment (e.g.\ Carta, Pulley, or a Kenya-specific equivalent)
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{The Advisor Agreement}

Every advisor, regardless of tier, is to sign a written Advisor Agreement before any equity is issued or any confidential information is shared. The agreement is to cover:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Scope of advisory services (specific, not vague)
  \item Equity grant amount and vesting schedule
  \item Confidentiality obligations
  \item IP assignment (any ideas or work product created in the advisory role belongs to EduReach)
  \item Term of the agreement and termination conditions
  \item Conflict of interest declaration (advisor confirms they are not advising a direct competitor)
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{What to Avoid with Advisors}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Giving equity to someone simply because they are well-known or have an impressive title --- equity must be earned
  \item Giving equity to an advisor who has not yet delivered any value (issue equity only after the cliff is reached)
  \item Having more than 6--8 advisors at any one time --- it becomes unmanageable and dilutes the pool unnecessarily
  \item Allowing an advisor to use their EduReach association to promote their own business without consent
  \item Forgetting to terminate advisor agreements and stop vesting when an advisor becomes inactive
\end{itemize}
